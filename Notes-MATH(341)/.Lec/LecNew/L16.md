# Coupling Eigenvalues 
Tradional textbook route: 
1. Write the characteristic equation $det(A-\lambda I)$ 
    - However this is numerically unstable w/ no closed formula for polynomials of degree $n \geq 5$

Before we get to algoritims used in computation, look at Rayleigh Quotient

# Rayleigh Quotient
*Assumption*: $A \in  \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$, [[Defs#semi-simple|Semi-Simple]]. 

For a vector $x \in  \mathbb{R}^n$, $RQ$ is a scalar:
$$ r(x) = \frac{x^TAx}{x^Tx} $$ 

If $x$ is an eigenvector of $A$, then $r(x) = \lambda$ corresponds to $x$Defs ❯ Semi-Simple

Question: Given $x$ what scalar of $\alpha$ "acts most like an eigenvalue" for $x$ with the sense; $\left\| Ax-\alpha x\right\|2$  is minimized?
- We want something like $(A-\lambda I)x = 0$ 

Answer: From the least squares formulation we can express the following.
$$
\begin{gathered}
\underset{\tiny{\text{To Find}}}{x \alpha} \approx \underset{\tiny{\text{Given}}}{Ax} \\
x^Tx \approx  x^TAx  \implies \alpha \approx \frac{x^TAx}{x^Tx} \approx r(x)
\end{gathered}
$$ 

The Rayleigh Quotient $\alpha$ is the best approximation of eigenvalue for $x$
$$
Ax = \lambda x \land r(x) := \frac{x^T A x}{x^T x} \implies r(x) = \frac{x^T \lambda x}{x^T x}
$$ 

## Power Itteration Algorithim
Generate a (random) vector $v^{(i)}$: $i :=$ denotes the itteration of the approximation. With $\left\| v^{(i)} \right\| = 1$.

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
\text{1. Find Inital Condition} & \forall~  ~k=1,2,\dots  \\
\text{2. Find $w$ } &w = Av^{(k-1)} \\
\text{3. Find $v$} &v^{(k)} = \frac{w}{\left\| w \right\|} \\
\text{4. Find Convergence} &\underset{\tiny{\text{Optionally compute the Rayleigh Quotient to see} convergence: }}{ \lambda^{(k)} = (v^{(k)})^T Av^{(k)} } 
\end{array}
$$ 

$$
\{ q_i \}^n_{i=1} := \text{ Basis for $\mathbb{R}^{n}$} \quad \quad v^{o} = a_1 q_1 + a_2 q_2 + \dots a_n q_n 
$$ 

In practice this means that $v^{(0)}$ contains the directions that are important for the computation.

So then $v^{(k)} = c_k A^k v^{(0)}$, where $c_k$ is from the product of the normalization

$$
\begin{aligned}
& = c_k (
a_1 \lambda_1^k q_1 +
a_2 \lambda_2^k q_2 + \dots 
a_n \lambda_n^k q_n +
) \\ \\
& = c_k \lambda_1^k 
\left(  
a_1 \left[ \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1})^k \right] q_1 +
a_2 \left[ \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1})^k \right] q_2 + \dots 
a_n \left[ \frac{\lambda_n}{\lambda_1})^k \right] q_n +
\right)
\end{aligned}
$$ 

Observe:
$$
= c_k \lambda_1^k 
\left(  
a_1 \left[ \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1})^k \right] q_1 +
a_2 \left[ \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1})^k \right] q_2 + \dots 
a_n \left[ \frac{\lambda_n}{\lambda_1})^k \right] q_n +
\right)
$$ 

$$
\begin{gathered}
x_n := \left(  
\left( \frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_1} \right)^k,
\left( \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} \right)^k,
\dots 
\left( \frac{\lambda_n}{\lambda_1} \right)^k
\right) \\
\lim_{k \to \infty} x_n = 0
\end{gathered}
$$ 

Thus we suppose: 
$$
\lambda_{k} < \lambda_{k-1} < \dots \lambda_{2} < \lambda_{1}
$$ 

# Inverse Iteration and Eigenvalue Shifts

Recall: Given the eigenvalue you can find the eigenvector. Likewise, if you have the eigenvector you can find the eigenvalue via the Rayleigh Quotient

Question: If $\frac{\lambda_1}{\lambda_2} \approx 1$ (convergence is small) can we improve it?
- Alternatively, can we look for other eigenvalues?

Answer: We can "amplify" the gap, or make any eigenvalue dominant, by using a "shift"

$$
\begin{gathered}
Ax = \lambda x |_{-\mu x} \\
Ax - \mu x = \lambda -\mu x \\
\implies (A - \mu I)x = (\lambda - \mu) x
\end{gathered}
$$ 

Assuming $\mu \approx \lambda \land \mu \neq \lambda$ s.t. $\exists~ (A-\mu I)^{-1}$. Note $0 < \lambda - \mu < 1$
$$
\implies (A-\mu I)^{-1}x = \underset{\text{Grows Large}}{\frac{1}{\lambda-\mu}} 
x
$$ 

Inverse Itteration Algorithim: 
$$
\begin{gathered}
\text{Define } v^{(0)} ~s.t.~ \left\| v^{(0)} \right\| = 1 \\
\forall~ k, 1,2,\dots \\
\text{Solve } \left( A - \mu I \right)w = v^{(k-1)}
\quad w := \left( A- \mu I \right)^{-1}v \\
\text{Find the Rayleigh Quotient } \lambda^{(k)} = \left( v^{(k)} \right)^T A v^{(k)} 
\end{gathered}
$$ 

"Note we don't want to work directly from inverses (Explain Why...)"

[!cor] 
1. Inverse itteration is known to perform well even if: $A-\mu I$ is close to singular (ill-conditioned). 
2. The convergence estimate
$$
\left\| v^{(k)} - \left[ \pm q_j \right] \right\| = \left( 
\left| 
\frac{\mu-\lambda_j}{\mu -\lambda_i}
 \right|^k
\right)
$$ 
So the convergence is still linear but $\mu$-helps. Where $\lambda_i$ is the closest eigenvalue to $\lambda_j$
