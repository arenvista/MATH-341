# Matrix Decompositions: EVD and SVD

### I. Eigenvalue Decomposition (EVD)
The standard Eigenvalue Decomposition applies to square matrices. Specifically, if a matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ is Symmetric Positive Definite (SPD), it can be decomposed as:
$$A = X \Lambda X^T$$

### II. Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)
What if $A$ is a rectangular matrix? The Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) generalizes the concept of eigendecomposition to any $n \times m$ matrix. 
$$A = U \Sigma V^T$$

> **Motivation:**
> 1. **Algebraic:** Every matrix is orthogonally equivalent to a diagonal matrix. 
> 2. **Geometric:** The range of the unit sphere under any $n \times m$ matrix transformation is a hyperellipse.

> **Definition: Orthogonal Equivalence**
> Two matrices $A, B \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ are said to be **equivalent** if there exist non-singular (square) matrices $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ and $Y \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$ such that $A = XBY$. 
> 
> If $X$ and $Y$ are orthogonal matrices (denoted $X \perp Y$), then $A$ and $B$ are said to be **orthogonally equivalent**.

---

### III. Formal Definition of SVD
Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ be a non-zero matrix with rank $r$. Then $A$ can be factored as $A = U \Sigma V^T$ (and consequently $A^T = V \Sigma^T U^T$), where:

* **$U \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$**: An orthogonal matrix. Its columns are the **left singular vectors**.
* **$V \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times m}$**: An orthogonal matrix. Its columns are the **right singular vectors**.
* **$\Sigma \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$**: A rectangular "diagonal" matrix containing the **singular values** ($\sigma$).

The matrix $\Sigma$ is structured such that the singular values are sorted in descending order:
$$
\Sigma =
\underbrace{
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \sigma_1 &          &        & \\
                 & \sigma_2 &        & \\
                 &          & \ddots & \\
                 &          &        & \sigma_r \\
                 &          &        &        &  0 \\
    \end{bmatrix} 
}_{
\quad \sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \dots \geq \sigma_r > 0
}
$$

### IV. Geometric Perspective
Let $S$ be the unit sphere in $\mathbb{R}^m$, and let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ where $m \leq n$.

The image $AS$ under the transformation $T(\vec{v}) := A \vec{v} = \sigma u$ forms a hyperellipse. 
* $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \dots$ represent the lengths of the principal semiaxes of the hyperellipse $AS$.
* $\sigma_i u_i$ is the $i^{th}$ largest semiaxis.

### V. Matrix Action and the Four Fundamental Subspaces
Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ be a non-zero matrix with rank $r$. By mapping between the orthonormal basis $v_1, \dots, v_m$ in $\mathbb{R}^m$ and the orthonormal basis $u_1, \dots, u_n$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$, the action of $A$ and $A^T$ is defined as:

$$
A \vec{v}_i = 
\begin{cases} 
\sigma_i u_i & i=1, 2, \dots, r \\ 
0 &  i=r+1, \dots, m 
\end{cases}
\quad \quad \quad 
A^T u_i = 
\begin{cases} 
\sigma_i v_i & i=1, 2, \dots, r \\ 
0 &  i=r+1, \dots, n 
\end{cases}
$$ 

Because of this specific mapping, the singular vectors form the bases for the **Four Fundamental Subspaces** of matrix $A$:

* **$\text{Range}(A)$**: $\text{Span}\{u_1, \dots, u_r\}$
* **$\text{Null}(A)$**: $\text{Span}\{v_{r+1}, \dots, v_m\}$
* **$\text{Range}(A^T)$**: $\text{Span}\{v_1, \dots, v_r\}$ (Also known as the Row Space)
* **$\text{Null}(A^T)$**: $\text{Span}\{u_{r+1}, \dots, u_n\}$ (Also known as the Left Null Space)

**Fundamental Subspace Theorem Recall:**
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \text{Range}(A) &= (\text{Null}(A^T))^\perp = \text{Range}(\text{Row}(A))\\
    \text{Range}(A^T) &= (\text{Null}(A))^\perp 
\end{aligned}
$$

Recall: 
$$
\left\| A \right\|_2 = max\left(
\frac{\left\| Ax \right\|_2}{\left\| x \right\|_2}
\right)
$$

Theorem: 
Let $A \in  \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ have singular values $0 < \sigma_r \leq  \dots \leq  \sigma_2 \leq \sigma_1$

$$
\left\| A_F \right\| = \left( \sum_{ij}^{} a_{ij}^2 \right)^{1/2} = \left( trace (A^TA) \right)^{1/2}
= \left\|\Sigma \right\|_F
$$


Corollary:
$$
\begin{gathered}
\left\| A \right\|_2 = \left\| A^T \right\|_2 \\
\text{Observe: } \left\| QA \right\|^2_F = trace(A^T \underbrace{Q^TQ}_{I} A) = trace(A^TA) = \left\| A \right\|_F
\end{gathered}
$$ 

Recall the spatial condition number:
$$
\kappa_2(A) = \left\| A \right\|_2 \left\| A^{-1} \right\|_2 $$ 

Thus:
$$
\begin{gathered}
A = U \Sigma V^T \implies A^{-1} = V^T \Sigma^{-1} U^{-1} = V \Sigma^{-1} U^T \\
0 < \sigma_1^{-1} \leq \dots \leq \sigma_{n-1}^{-1} \leq \sigma_n^{-1}  \implies \left\| A^{-1} \right\|_2 = \sigma_n^{-1}
\end{gathered}
$$

Note:
$$ \kappa(A^TA) = (\kappa(A))^2 $$
