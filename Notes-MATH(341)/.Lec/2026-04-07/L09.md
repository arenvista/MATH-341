# Least Squares

$$
	\begin{bmatrix}
		1 & 1  \\
		1 & -1 \\
		1 & 1  \\
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		x_1 \\ x_2
	\end{bmatrix} =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		2 \\ 1 \\ 3
	\end{bmatrix}
	\iff
	x_1 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} +
	x_2 \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} +
	= \begin{bmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix}
$$

## Geomentric Perspective

$$ \vec{v_1}, \vec{v_2} \text{ span a plane} \implies A \vec{x} = \vec{b} \text{ (only if $\vec{b}$ is within the plane)} $$

$$
\begin{gather}
    \text{In general in $\mathbb{R}^n$ with $A \in  \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$, we have $ b-A \overline{x} \perp \{ Ax | x \in  \mathbb{R}^n \}$} \\
\equiv (Ax)^T(b-A \overline{x}) = 0 \\
\equiv x^TA^T(b-A \overline{x}) = 0 \\
\text{which as to hold $\forall~ x \in  \mathbb{R}^n$} \\
\implies A^T(b-A \overline{x}) = 0 \quad \text{ and } \quad A^TA \overline{x} = A^Tb \tiny{\text{ normal eqs.}} \\
\text{Which gives the least squares solution of the system $Ax=b$}
\end{gather}
$$

## Application (Fitting Data)

$$ \text{Goal: } min \left( \sum\_{i=1}^{N} \delta x_i^2 \right) := \text{ The error between $(\hat{y}-y$})$$

> [!def] Algorithim:
>
> 1. Given a set of all data points $\{ (x_i,y_i) | x \in X, y \in  Y \}$
> 2. Choose a model to fit the dataa (linear, ...)
> 3. Subsitute the data into the model
> 4. Solve the normal equations
> 5. Estimate the error, possibly repeat

$$
	\begin{gather}
		\text{Consider datapoints: } \{ (-1,1), (0,0), (1,0), (2,-2) \} \\
		\text{Rewrite as linear system ${\color{green}a}x + {\color{red}b} =\hat{y}$: } \\
		\equiv
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\color{green}{1} & \color{red}{1} \\
			\color{green}{0} & \color{red}{1} \\
			\color{green}{1} & \color{red}{1} \\
			\color{green}{2} & \color{red}{1}
		\end{bmatrix}
		\begin{bmatrix}
			x_1 \\ x_2
		\end{bmatrix} =
		\begin{bmatrix}
			1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \\ 0
		\end{bmatrix} \\
		\text{Place in the normal equation: $A^TAx=A^Tb$} \\
		\begin{bmatrix}
			6 & 2 \\
			2 & 4
		\end{bmatrix}
		\begin{bmatrix} a \\b \end{bmatrix} =
		\begin{bmatrix}
			-5 \\ -1
		\end{bmatrix}
		\implies
		\begin{bmatrix}
			a = -0.9 \\ b = 0.2
		\end{bmatrix}
		\implies  \hat{y} = -0.9x + 0.2 \\
		\text{Calculate the error (root mean sqared) } := \overline{r} := b-A \overline{x} \\
		\left\| \overline{r} \right\|_2 = \sqrt{ r_1^2 r_2^2 \dots } \\
		\text{Compute roots to minimize the error: }  RMSE = \sqrt{\frac{SE}{n}}
	\end{gather}
$$

Consider: othere model with positive data with ....

$$
	\hat{y} = c_1 + c_2 cos^2(x)t + c_3 sin(2x)t + c_4 cos(4xt) + \dots
$$

Note: positive nonlinear models - may introduce us to data linearization:

1. Expoenential Model: $y=c_1 e ^{c_2 t}$

$$
	\begin{align}
		y        & = c_1 e ^{c_2 t}    \\
		ln(y)    & =  ln(c_1e^{c_2) t} \\
		ln(y)    & =  ln(c_1) + (c_2t) \\
		\equiv Y & =  k + c_2t
	\end{align}
$$

2. Power Law Model: $y = c_1 t^{c_2}$
   $$
   	\begin{align}
   		         & \text{Apply similar logic as above: }  \\
   		\equiv Y & =  k + c_2t \equiv Y-ln(t) = k + c_2 t
   	\end{align}
   $$

What can we use instead of normal equations to solve least squares approximation?
$$ A \cdot \text{ Possibly } QR \text{ or } SVD $$

$$
	\begin{gather}
		QR \implies  \text{minimize: }
		\left\| Ax - b \right\|_2 \\
		= \left\| QRx-bx \right\|_2
		= \left\| Q^TQRx - Q^T \right\|_2 \\
		= \left\| Rx-Q^Tb \right\|_2 = \left\| r \right\|_2
	\end{gather}
$$
$$
	\begin{gather}
		\text{Witout the norm: } r = Rx-d \\
		\begin{bmatrix}
			r_1     \\
			r_2     \\
			\vdots  \\
			r_n     \\
			r_{n+1} \\
			r_k     \\
		\end{bmatrix}
		\begin{bmatrix}
			r_{1,1} & \dots  & r_{1,m} \\
			\vdots  & \ddots & \vdots  \\
			r_{n,1} &        & r_{m,n}
		\end{bmatrix} =
		\begin{bmatrix}
			d_1     \\
			d_2     \\
			\vdots  \\
			d_n     \\
			d_{n+1} \\
			d_k     \\
		\end{bmatrix}
	\end{gather}
$$
