# Singular Value Decomposition

Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$. The Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) of $A$ is:

$$ A = \underset{\text{Orthogonal}}{U} \ \underset{\text{Diagonal (possibly not square)}}{\Sigma} \ \underset{\text{Orthogonal}}{V^T} $$

where:
- $U$ is an $m \times m$ orthogonal matrix (columns are the left singular vectors).
- $\Sigma$ is an $m \times n$ diagonal matrix (with non-negative entries called \emph{singular values}).
- $V$ is an $n \times n$ orthogonal matrix (columns are the right singular vectors).

---

### Expression as a Sum of Rank-One Matrices

$A$ can be written as the sum of rank-one matrices:

$$ A = \sum_{j=1}^{r} \sigma_j u_j v_j^T $$

Here, $\sigma_j$ are the singular values, $u_j$ and $v_j$ are the $j$th left and right singular vectors, and $r = \text{rank}(A)$.

#### Partial Sums (Rank-$\nu$ Approximation)
Let $A_\nu$ be the partial sum:

$$ A_\nu = \sum_{j=1}^{\nu} \sigma_j u_j v_j^T $$

$A_\nu$ captures as much information about $A$ as possible with rank at most $\nu$.

---

> [!thm] Theorem: Rank from Singular Values
> The rank of $A$ is $r$, the number of nonzero singular values.

> [!thm] Theorem: Range and Nullspace
> $$
> \begin{gathered}
> \text{Range}(A) = \text{span} \{ u_1, \dots, u_r \} \\
> \text{Null}(A) = \text{span} \{ v_{r+1}, \dots, v_n \}
> \end{gathered}
> $$

> [!thm] Theorem: Best Low-Rank Approximation (Eckart–Young)
> For all $0 \leq \nu \leq r$, define $A_\nu$ as above. If $\nu = p = \min \{ m, n \}$, let $\sigma_{\nu+1} = 0$. Then
> $$
> \|A - A_\nu\|_z = \inf_{\text{rank}(B)\leq\nu} \|A - B\|_z = \sigma_{\nu+1}
> $$
> Thus, $A_\nu$ is as close as possible to $A$ (in the $z$-norm) among all matrices of rank at most $\nu$.
>
> > [!eli5]
> > This is the Eckart–Young theorem:
> > - Among all matrices $B$ of rank at most $\nu$, the best approximation to $A$ (in the $z$-norm) is $A_\nu$ (constructed from the top $\nu$ singular values and vectors).
> > - The smallest possible error is $\sigma_{\nu+1}$.
> > - If $A$ is already full rank ($\nu = p$), then $\sigma_{\nu+1} = 0$ and the approximation is exact.

> [!cor] Corollary: Full Rank Stability
> Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ have full rank and singular values $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r > 0$, $r = \min\{m, n\}$. If $B \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ and $\|A - B\|_z < \sigma_r$, then $B$ also has full rank.

---

### Numerical Rank
The **numerical rank** of $A$ is $k$ if
$$
\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_k \geq \epsilon \gg \sigma_{k+1}
$$
where $\epsilon$ (the precision threshold) is typically $\approx 10^{-16}$ for double-precision IEEE arithmetic.

Note: $\sigma_1 = \|A\|_z$

# SVD v.s. Eigenvalue Decomposition 

## Eigenvalue Decomposition
If the columns of $X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ contain linearly indepdendent eigenvectors of $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$. 
Then $A = X \Lambda X^{-1}$, in this case $A$ is said to be diagonalizable. 

## SVD
In SVD $\forall~ A, A = U \Sigma V^T$. 

## Differences 

| Feature         | SVD                                                        | Eigenvalue Decomposition                                                      |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Generality      | More general, uses two bases $U, V$                        | Uses just one basis $X$                                                       |
| Orthogonality   | Uses orthogonal bases                                       | Eigenvectors may/may not be orthogonal; orthogonal iff $A$ is single, positive, definite |
| Existence       | All matrices have an SVD                                    | Only exists if $A$ is square and geometric multiplicity equals algebraic multiplicity |


# Theorem

The nonzero singular values of $A$ are the square roots of the nonzero eigenvalues of $A^TA$ or $AA^T$.

*Note*: $A^TA$ and $AA^T$ have the same nonzero eigenvalues. Use the form that minimizes the matrix size.

## Proof

Let $A = U \Sigma V^T$ be the singular value decomposition (SVD) of $A$.

$$
\begin{align*}
A^TA &= (U \Sigma V^T)^T (U \Sigma V^T) \\
     &= V \Sigma^T U^T U \Sigma V^T \\
     &= V (\Sigma^T \Sigma) V^T
\end{align*}
$$

Thus, $A^TA$ and $\Sigma^T\Sigma$ are similar matrices, so they have the same eigenvalues.

> **Definition:** Matrices $A$ and $B$ are similar if there exists a nonsingular matrix $M$ such that $A = MBM^{-1}$.

> **Theorem:** Similar matrices have the same eigenvalues, since $M$ changes the basis.

# Least Squares Solution via SVD

Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ (non-square), $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and $r = \operatorname{rank}(A)$. Consider the system: $Ax = b$
where $x \in \mathbb{R}^m$.

We seek $x$ minimizing $\left\| b - Ax \right\|_2$ (least-squares), with the additional requirement that among all solutions, $x$ has minimal $\left\| x \right\|_2$.

Suppose the singular value decomposition (SVD) of $A$ is given by:
$$
A = U \Sigma V^T
$$
where
$$
\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\Sigma} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix},
\quad \hat{\Sigma} = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \dots, \sigma_r)
$$

### Solution Strategy

Because $U$ is orthogonal ($U^TU = I$),
$$
\left\| b-Ax \right\|_2 = \left\| U^T(b-Ax) \right\|_2 = \left\| U^T b - \Sigma (V^T x) \right\|_2
$$
Define:
- $c = U^T b = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{c} \\ d \end{bmatrix}$, where $\hat{c} \in \mathbb{R}^r$, $d \in \mathbb{R}^{n-r}$
- $y = V^T x = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{y} \\ z \end{bmatrix}$, where $\hat{y} \in \mathbb{R}^r$, $z \in \mathbb{R}^{m-r}$

Then:
$$
c - \Sigma y = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{c} \\ d \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\Sigma} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \hat{y} \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{c} - \hat{\Sigma}\hat{y} \\ d \end{bmatrix}
$$
Minimizing $\left\| c - \Sigma y \right\|_2$ gives:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^r |\hat{c}_i - \sigma_i \hat{y}_i|^2 + \sum_{i=r+1}^{n} |d_i|^2
$$
This is minimized for:
$$
\hat{y}_i = \frac{\hat{c}_i}{\sigma_i},\quad i = 1, \dots, r
$$

If $r < m$, set $z = 0$ to minimize $\|x\|_2$ (minimum norm solution).

### Algorithm Steps

1. Compute $c = U^T b = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{c} \\ d \end{bmatrix}$.
2. Set $\hat{y} = \hat{\Sigma}^{-1} \hat{c}$.
3. For $r < m$, set $z = 0$ (a vector of zeros of length $m-r$).
4. Form $y = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{y} \\ z \end{bmatrix}$.
5. Find the minimizer $x = V y$.

# Least Squares Solution via SVD

Let $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$ (non-square), $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and $r = \operatorname{rank}(A)$. Consider the system: $Ax = b$
where $x \in \mathbb{R}^m$.

$$
A = U \Sigma V^T
$$
where
$$
\Sigma = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\Sigma} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix},
\quad \hat{\Sigma} = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \dots, \sigma_r)
$$

### Solution Strategy

Because $U$ is orthogonal ($U^TU = I$),
$$
\left\| b-Ax \right\|_2 = \left\| U^T(b-Ax) \right\|_2 = \left\| U^T b - \Sigma (V^T x) \right\|_2
$$
Define:
- $c = U^T b = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{c} \\ d \end{bmatrix}$, where $\hat{c} \in \mathbb{R}^r$, $d \in \mathbb{R}^{n-r}$
- $y = V^T x = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{y} \\ z \end{bmatrix}$, where $\hat{y} \in \mathbb{R}^r$, $z \in \mathbb{R}^{m-r}$

Then:
$$
c - \Sigma y = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{c} \\ d \end{bmatrix} - \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\Sigma} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \hat{y} \\ z \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{c} - \hat{\Sigma}\hat{y} \\ d \end{bmatrix}
$$
Minimizing $\left\| c - \Sigma y \right\|_2$ gives:
$$
\sum_{i=1}^r |\hat{c}_i - \sigma_i \hat{y}_i|^2 + \sum_{i=r+1}^{n} |d_i|^2

This is minimized for:
$$
\hat{y}_i = \frac{\hat{c}_i}{\sigma_i},\quad i = 1, \dots, r
$$

If $r < m$, set $z = 0$ to minimize $\|x\|_2$ (minimum norm solution).

### Algorithm Steps

1. Compute $c = U^T b = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{c} \\ d \end{bmatrix}$.
2. Set $\hat{y} = \hat{\Sigma}^{-1} \hat{c}$.
3. For $r < m$, set $z = 0$ (a vector of zeros of length $m-r$).
4. Form $y = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{y} \\ z \end{bmatrix}$.
5. Find the minimizer $x = V y$.

This note explains how to solve the least-squares problem $Ax = b$ using the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD). Here's a step-by-step explanation:

**Problem:**  
Given a possibly non-square matrix $A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times m}$, and a vector $b \in \mathbb{R}^n$, find an $x \in \mathbb{R}^m$ that minimizes the Euclidean norm $\|b - Ax\|_2$ (i.e., makes $Ax$ as close as possible to $b$), and among all such $x$, pick the one with the smallest $\|x\|_2$.

**Step 1: SVD of $A$**  
SVD lets us write:
$$
A = U \Sigma V^T
$$
where:
- $U$ and $V$ are orthogonal matrices.
- $\Sigma$ is diagonal, with non-negative values $\sigma_i$ (the singular values) in the diagonal of the top $r \times r$ submatrix ($r = \operatorname{rank}(A)$). The rest is zeros.

**Step 2: Change of Variables**  
Multiplying the error $b - Ax$ by $U^T$ (which preserves the length because $U$ is orthogonal):
$$
\|b - Ax\|_2 = \|U^T(b - Ax)\|_2 = \|U^T b - \Sigma V^T x\|_2
$$
Call $c = U^T b$ (so $c$ is $b$ in the "rotated" coordinates), $y = V^T x$ (so $x = Vy$).

**Step 3: Subdivide Vectors**  
Break $c$ and $y$ into parts based on the rank:
- $\hat{c}, \hat{y}$ : First $r$ components.
- $d, z$ : Remaining $n-r$ and $m-r$ components.

So $\Sigma y = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\Sigma} \hat{y} \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}$.

**Step 4: Minimize the Error**  
To minimize $\|c - \Sigma y\|_2$, the best you can do for the top $r$ parts is to set each $\hat{y}_i = \hat{c}_i/\sigma_i$, and for the $z$ part (if $m > r$), choose $z = 0$ (for minimal $\|x\|_2$).

**Step 5: Recover $x$**  
Now you have $y = [\hat{y}; z]$, so you recover $x$ via $x = V y$.

**Summary Algorithm:**
1. Compute $U, \Sigma, V$ (the SVD of $A$).
2. Compute $c = U^T b$.
3. For the first $r$ components, set $\hat{y}_i = \hat{c}_i/\sigma_i$; for the remaining components, set them to $0$.
4. Build $y$ from $\hat{y}$ and $z=0$.
5. Compute $x = V y$.

$$ \Sigma^{\dagger} = \begin{bmatrix} \hat{\Sigma}^{-1} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} $$

If $A = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \sigma_i u_i v_i^T$ then $A^{\dagger} = \sum_{i-1}^{r} \sigma_i^{-1}v_i u_i^T$.
