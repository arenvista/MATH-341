# Gram-Schmitt Algorithim

_Usecase:_ Gram-Schmitt is particularly useful for $QR$ factorization.

Objective: Transform an _orthgonal basis_ $B = \{ x_1 x_2, ..., x_n \}$ into an \_orthonormal basis $U = \{u_1, u_2, ..., u_n \}$

## Approach (Induct.):

$$k(1):= ~u_1 = \frac{x_1}{|x_1|}$$

Suppose $Span\{u_1, u_2, ..., u_n\} = Span \{ x_1, x_2, ..., x_n \}$.

**_Goal:_** We want to add $U_{k+1}$ to $O_k$ so that $Span(O_{k+1}) = Span(B_{k+1})$

We have $\{ x_1, x_2, ..., x_k, x_{k+1} \}$. We want the set $u_k+1$

### Recall: Fouier Augmentation

$$ x*k = \sum*{n=1}^{k} \langle u_i | u_k \rangle u_i $$

$$
	\implies  u_{k+1} =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		x_{k+1} = \sum_{n=1}^{{k+1}} \langle u_i | u_{k+1} \rangle u_i \\
		\hline                                                         \\
		|x_{k+1} = \sum_{n=1}^{{k+1}} \langle u_i | u_{k+1} \rangle u_i|
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

Therefore:

$$
	u_k =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		x_k - \sum_{i=1}^{k} \\ \hline \frac{(u_i^T x_k)u_i}{|(u_i^T x_k)u_i|}
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

## In Matrix Notation:

$$
	U_k = \{ u_1, u_2, u_{k-1} \} \quad \text{\textit{max(k-1)}}
$$

$$
	\text{Choose: }
	U_k^T x_k =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		U_1^T \\
		U_2^T \\
		...   \\
		U_k^T \\
	\end{bmatrix} =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		u_1^Tx_1 \\
		u_2^Tx_2 \\
		...      \\
		u_k^Tx_k \\
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
	U_k U_k^T x_k =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		u_1 & u_2 & ... & u_{k-1}
	\end{bmatrix}
	\begin{bmatrix}
		u_1^Tx_1 \\
		u_2^Tx_2 \\
		...      \\
		u_k^Tx_k \\
	\end{bmatrix} =
	\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} u_i(u_i^T x_k) = \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (u_i^T x_k)u_i
$$

$$ \tiny{\text{Note $u_i(u_i^T x_k)$ is an inner product; thus is a scalar }} $$

Then:

$$
	d_k = \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (u_i^T x_k)u_i = x_k - U_k U_k^T x_k = (I - U_k U_k^T) x_k
$$

$$
	\implies u_k =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		(I - U_k U_k^T) x_k \\
		\hline              \\
		|(I - U_k U_k^T) x_k|
	\end{bmatrix}
	\quad \text{Holds } \forall ~k, k:=1,2,...,n
$$

## Generalized Algorithim

$$
	\begin{aligned}
		 & k(1):= ~u_1 = \frac{x_1}{|x_1|}             \\
		 & \text{For } k=2,...,n                       \\
		 & u_k = x_k - \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (u_i^T x_k)u_i \\
	\end{aligned}
$$

$$ \tiny\text{\textit{{Numerically Unstable; (Stable ver. via "Modified Gram Schmitt")}}} $$

# QR Factorization

Suppose we have a finite matrix:
$$ A = \{ a_1, a_2, ..., a_n \} \xrightarrow{T: \text{ A to orthonormal basis } Q} \{ q_1, q_2, ..., q_n \} \equiv T: A \xrightarrow{Gram-Schmitt} Q $$

$$ q_1 = \frac{a_1}{|a_1|} $$

$$
	\implies q_k =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\sum_{n=1}^{k-1} \langle q_n | a_k \rangle q_n \\
		\hline                                         \\
		|\sum_{n=1}^{k-1} \langle q_n | a_k \rangle q_n |
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
	a_k = \sum_{n=1}^{k-1} <q_n|a_k>q_n + x_kq_k
$$

$$ \text{In matrix form} $$

$$
	\tiny{
		\begin{aligned}
			\begin{bmatrix}
				a_1 & a_2 & ... & a_k &
			\end{bmatrix} =
			\begin{bmatrix}
				q_1 & q_2 & ... & q_n
			\end{bmatrix}
			\cdot
			\begin{bmatrix}
				|u_1| & <q_1 | a_2> & <q_1 | a_3> & ...         &       & <q_1|a_n> \\
				0     & |u_2|       & <q_2 | a_3> &                                 \\
				0     &             & |u_3|       & <q_3 | a_4>                     \\
				...   &             &             & ...         &                   \\
				0     &             &             &             & |u_n|             \\
			\end{bmatrix}
		\end{aligned}
	}
$$

$$ A*{m \times n} = Q*{m \times n} \cdot R\_{n \times n} \quad \tiny\text{Note: $R$ is an upper right triangle postive diagonal matrix (m must be greater than or equal to n)} $$

| QR                                                                                              | LU                                             |

| If it exits it is uniqe                                                                         | Same                                           |
| Exists for rectantular A w/ lin. indep. cols                                                    | A has to be a square matrix (nonsingular)      |

| Backsubsititions                                                                                | 2x Backsubsititiions                           |

# Modified Gram Schmitt

Recall (single vector projector):

$$
	u_k =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		(I-U_k U_k^T)x_k \\
		\hline
		|(I-U_k U_k^T)x_k|
	\end{bmatrix}
$$

Let $E_1 = I$ and $i \geq 1$, $E_i = I-(u_{i-1}u_{i-1})^T$

$$
	\begin{align}
		\implies E_3E_2E_1 = (I-u_2u_2^T)(I-u_1u_1^T)I                           \\
		= I-u_1u_1^T-u_2u_2^T-u_2u_2^Tu_1u_1^T \quad \text{Note: $u_2^Tu_1 = 0$} \\
	\end{align}
$$

$$
	\text{Generally Expressed: }   E_kE_{k-1}...E_1 = I-U_kU_{k-1}^T = I-u_1u_1^T...-u_{k-1}u_{k-1}^T
$$

$$ \tiny{\text{Note actions in the numerator can be implamented using this* } E_kE_{k-1}...E_1} $$
