# Nonlinear Least Squares

## Problem setup

Suppose we want to solve a system of nonlinear equations

$$
	\begin{aligned}
		r_1(x_1, \dots, x_n) & = 0    \\
		                     & \vdots \\
		r_n(x_1, \dots, x_n) & = 0
	\end{aligned}
$$

where each $r_i(x)$ is called a **residual**.

### Idea

Instead of solving all equations directly, we measure how far we are from a solution by defining an error function.

## Error function

Define

$$ E(x*1, \dots, x_n) = \frac{1}{2} \sum*{i=1}^n r_i(x)^2. $$

### Why this helps

- Each residual $r_i(x)$ is squared, so large errors are penalized more.
- If all residuals are zero, then $E(x)=0$, which is the minimum possible value.
- So solving the nonlinear system is equivalent to minimizing $E(x)$.

## Goal: minimize the error

To minimize $E$, we set its gradient equal to zero:

$$ \nabla E(x) = F(x) = 0. $$

This gives a system of $n$ equations in $n$ variables.

---

## Useful gradient identities

> [!tip] Recall:
>
> 1. For scalar functions $u,v$,
>    $$ \nabla(u^T v) = v^T Du + u^T Dv $$
> 2. For a matrix $A$ and vector $v$,
>    $$ \nabla(Av) = A\,Dv + \sum\_{i=1}^n v_i\,Da_i $$

Here, $D$ denotes the Jacobian/derivative operator.

---

## Deriving the gradient of the error

Let

$$
	r(x) =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		r_1(x) \\
		\vdots \\
		r_n(x)
	\end{bmatrix}.
$$

Then the error can be written compactly as

$$ E(x) = \frac{1}{2} r(x)^T r(x). $$

Now differentiate:

$$ F(x) = \nabla E(x) = \frac{1}{2} \nabla\big(r^T r\big). $$

Using the product rule,

$$ \nabla(r^T r) = r^T Dr + r^T Dr. $$

So,

$$ F(x) = \frac{1}{2}\big(r^T Dr + r^T Dr\big) = r^T Dr. $$

### Simple interpretation

- $r(x)$ tells us the current residuals.
- $Dr$ tells us how those residuals change when $x$ changes.
- Therefore, $r^TDr$ measures how the total error changes, which is exactly the gradient.

---

## Column-vector form

Since gradients are usually written as column vectors, we often consider

$$ F(x)^T $$

as a column vector and solve

$$ F(x)^T = 0. $$

This is a system of length $n$, matching the number of unknowns.


Here
$$ F^T = (r^TDr)^T = (Dr)^Tr $$
and

$$
	DF^T = D((Dr)^T r) \overset{2.}{=} (Dr)^TDr + \sum_{i=1}^{n} r_iDc_i \quad \tiny{\text{\textit{$c_i$ are the columns of $(Dr)^T$}}}
$$

Here

> [!?]- What is $Dr$?
>
> $$
> 	\underbrace{D}_{\text{\tiny{Jacobian Matrix}}} \vec{r} = \begin{bmatrix}
> 		\delta r_1 \\
> 		\vdots     \\
> 		\delta r_1 \\
> \end{bmatrix} =
> 	\begin{bmatrix}
> 		\frac{\partial r_1}{\partial x_1} & \dots  & \frac{\partial r_1}{\partial x_n} \\
> 		\vdots                            & \ddots &                                   \\
> 		\frac{\partial r_n}{\partial x_1} & \dots  & \frac{\partial r_n}{\partial x_n}
> \end{bmatrix}
> $$

$$
	Dr_i =
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\nabla \cdot \frac{\partial r_1}{\partial x_1} \\
		\vdots                                         \\
		\nabla \cdot \frac{\partial r_i}{\partial x_n}
	\end{bmatrix}
\underbrace{
	\begin{bmatrix}
		\frac{\partial^2 r_i}{\partial x_1 \partial x_1} & \dots  & \frac{\partial^2 r_i}{\partial x_n \partial x_1} \\
		\vdots                                           & \ddots &                                                  \\
		\frac{\partial^2 r_i}{\partial x_1 \partial x_n} & \dots  & \frac{\partial^2 r_i}{\partial x_n \partial x_n}
	\end{bmatrix}
}_{\text{Hassian Matrix of $r_i$}}
$$

> [!def] Hessian/Jacobian
> * **Jacobian**: Matrix of first-order partial derivatives of a vector-valued function
> * **Hessian**: Matrix of second-order partial derivatives of a scalar-valued function

Next denote $A = Dr$

$$ DF^T = \underbrace{A^TA}_{\tiny{\text{First-Order Term}}} + \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{n} r_i H_{r_i}}_{\tiny{\text{Second-Order Term}}} \approx A^TA $$ 

Note: $\approx A^TA$ using the first-order term as in Newton's method.

# Gauss-Newton's Method
Using the components we derrive we can construct an algorithim from an application of Newton's Method:

> [!pf]- Recall: Newton's Method
> **Identify an inital vector**
> $$ \text{Let } x_0 \text{ the inital vector} $$ 
> **Solve for $-F(x_k)$**
> $$ DF(x_k) \Delta x = -F(x_k) $$ 
> **Update $x_{k+1}$**
> $$ x_{k+1} = x_k + \Delta x $$ 

> [!tip] Gauss-Newton's Method
> **Identify an inital vector**
> $$ \text{Let } x_0 \text{ the inital vector} $$ 
> **Set $A$**
> $$ A = Dr(x_k) $$ 
> **Solve for $-A^Tr(x_k)$**
> $$ A^TA \Delta x = A^Tr(x_k) $$ 
> **Update $x_k+1$**
> $$ x_{k+1} = x_k + \Delta x $$
