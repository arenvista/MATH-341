We have the [[L05#Four Fundamental Subspaces]]

Consider $A \in \mathbb{R}^M{m \times n}: I \text{ take } x \in R(A)^T$ that is:

$$
	\begin{align}
		\forall ~y \in \mathbb{R}^n, ~0 =  \langle x|Ay \rangle  & \quad \text{("|" represents inner product)}                 \\
		                                         & = (Ay)^Tx                                                   \\
		                                         & = y^TA^Tx =  \langle y, \forall ~y \in \mathbb{R}^n | A^Tx \rangle  = 0     \\
		                                         & \implies A^Tx=0 \implies x \in Nul(A^T)                     \\
		                                         & \implies R(A)^\perp = Nul(A^T)                              \\
		\text{Take } x \in R(A)^{T \perp}        & \quad ... \text{ Similarly as above (replace $A$ by $A^T$)} \\
		                                         & \text{(that is )} \implies  R(A^T)^\perp = Nul(A)
	\end{align}
$$

# Relationship of Orthogonality and Fundamental Subspaces

[!thm] Relationship of Orthogonality and Fundamental Subspaces
Recall: Dims $Nul(A)$ = Num of Free Vars = Nullity = Num Cols - Rank

$$
	\begin{align}
		\text{dim = r} \quad    & R(A^T) \perp N(A) \quad \text{dims = r}   \\
		\text{dims = n-r} \quad & R(A) \perp N(A^T) \quad \text{dims = m-r}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
& \mathbb{R}^n = N(A) \oplus N(A)^T = N(A) \oplus R(A^T) \quad \text{using II}    \\
& \mathbb{R}^m = R(A) \oplus R(A)^\perp = R(A) \oplus N(A^T) \quad \text{using I}
\end{align}
$$

"The best basis is the orthonormal basis - likely sparse"
[!def] Fourier Expansion ^FouierExpansion
If $B = \{ x_1, x_2, ..., x_n \}$ is an orthonormal basis has an inner product space.

[!tip] Orthonomal Basis
$$ u_i \perp u_j \quad i \neq j \quad ||u_i|| = 1 $$
Every vector in an orthonormal vector is $\perp$ to all other vectors and has a length of one.

[!def] Generalized Fourier Expansion of x
This can be written as:
$$ x =  \langle u_1 | x \rangle  u_1 +  \langle u_2|x \rangle  u_2  ~+...  +  \langle u_n|x \rangle u_x $$

[!def] Fourier Coefficents $ \langle u_i|x \rangle $:
$$  \langle u_i|x \rangle  \text{is an orthogonal projection of $x$ onto the space spanned by $u_i$} $$

$$
	 \langle u_i|u_j \rangle =u_i^Tu_j= \delta_{ij} \quad
	\delta_{i,j}=\begin{cases}
		i=j \implies 1 \\
		i \neq j \implies  0
	\end{cases}
$$


We can place vector $u_{i,j}$, is $... n$ as shows of a matrix $Q = [ \vec{u_1}, \vec{u_2}, ..., \vec{u_n} ]$ 

[!tip] Orthogonal Matricies have orthonormal columns.

$$
Q^TQ = 
\begin{bmatrix} u_1^T \\ u_2^T \\ ...\\ u_n^T \\ \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} u_1, u_2, ..., u_n \end{bmatrix} = I
$$ 

$$ \therefore \text{If } Q \text{ is sparse } \implies Q^T = Q^{-1} $$ 


[!cor] Nomenclature
If $Q$ is rectangular - _"it is a matrix with orthonormal columns"_
If $Q$ is square - _"it is an orthogonal matrix"_
^Nomenclature

[!def] Orthogonal Matricies
A matrix, $A$, is orthogonal $\iff ||Qx|| = ||x||$. 
$$ \text{\textit{"In other words the size of the vector does not change."}} $$ 
$$ ||Qx||^2 =  \langle Qx|Qx \rangle  = (Qx)^TQx = x^TQ^TQx = x^Tx = ||x||^2 $$ 
Examples of Q: rotators, reflectors, permutation matricies, etc.

[!def] Orthogonal Projector
In particular consider: (outer product) 
$$
    P_u = u_i u_i^T = \text{ orthogonal projector onto } Span(u_i)
$$ 

Note: If $||u_i|| \neq 1$, take $P_u = \frac{u_iu_i^T}{u_i^Tu_i}$

Consider: 
$$
    P_u x = (u_i u_i^T) x = u_i u_i^T x = u_i  \langle u_i|x \rangle  =  \langle u_i|x \rangle u_i 
$$
