# Cholesky Factorization

> [!def] Cholesky Factorization
>
> An [LU Factorization](1770301034-TEDP.md) of _Symmetric Positive Definite (SPD)_ matricies.
>
> $$
> 	\text{nonsigular } A \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}
> $$
>
> ## Symmetric
>
> > [!def] Symmetric
> > Note if $A$ is said to be **symmetric**:
> > $$ A = A^T $$
>
> ## Positive Definite
>
> > [!def] Positive Definite
> > Note if $A$ is said to be **positive definite**:
> >
> > $$
> > 	\begin{align}
> > & v^TAv=0                                     \\
> > & \forall ~v \in \mathbb{R}^n - \{ \vec{0} \}
> > \end{align}
> > $$
>
> > [!thm] For such $A$ (SPD) there exists a unique factorization:
> >
> > $$
> >     A = R^TR
> > $$
> >
> > Where $R$ is the upper triangular matrix, and all $r_{i,i} > 0$ (all diagonal entries are positive)
> >
> > [!tip] Note: $R...$ is called the Cholesky Factor

## Block Matricies

> [!def] Block Matricies
> Example:
>
> $$
>     \begin{bmatrix}
>         A_{1,1} & A_{1,2} \\
>         A_{2,1} & A_{2,2}
> \end{bmatrix}
>     \begin{bmatrix}
>         X_{1,1} & X_{1,2} \\
>         X_{2,1} & X_{2,2}
> \end{bmatrix}  =
>     \begin{bmatrix}
>         B_{1,1} & B_{1,2} \\
>         B_{2,1} & B_{2,2}
> \end{bmatrix}
> $$
>
> Observe $A,X,B$ are called _submatricies_.
>
> > [!tip] Note we must use _conforming partitioning_; the dims of the matricies being _multiplied_ must match

## Example: Cholesky Factorization

> [!ex] Example: Cholesky Factorization
> Let us consider the following:
>
> > [!case] Case A: $A,B$ form a block diagonal
> >
> > $$
> > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > 		A & 0 \\
> > 		0 & B
> > \end{bmatrix}^{-1} =
> > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > 		A^{-1} & 0      \\
> > 		0      & B^{-1}
> > \end{bmatrix}
> > $$
> >
> > This holds, provided $A,B$ are invertible.
>
> > [!case] Case B: $A,B,C$ form a block upper triangular
> >
> > $$
> > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > 		A & C \\
> > 		0 & B
> > \end{bmatrix}^{-1} =
> > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > 		A^{-1} & -A^{-1} C B^{-1} \\
> > 		0      & B^{-1}
> > \end{bmatrix}
> > $$
> >
> > > [!ex] Case B Expanded
> > > Consider:
> > >
> > > $$
> > > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > > 		A & C \\
> > > 		0 & B
> > > \end{bmatrix}^{-1}
> > > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > > 		A^{-1} & -A^{-1} C B^{-1} \\
> > > 		0      & B^{-1}
> > > \end{bmatrix} =
> > > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > > 		AA^{-1} + C0 & A(-A^{-1}CB^{-1})+CB^{-1} \\
> > > 		0A{_1} + B0  & 0(-A^{-1}) + BB^{-1}
> > > \end{bmatrix} =
> > > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > > 		I & 0 \\
> > > 		0 & I
> > > \end{bmatrix}
> > > $$
>
> > [!case] Case C: No 0 Block
> > Consider:
> >
> > $$
> > D =
> > \begin{bmatrix}
> >     A & C \\
> >     R & B
> > \end{bmatrix}^{-1} = ~~?
> > $$
> >
> > > [!thm] Existence of Solution:
> > > When the corresponding inversees exist
> > > Q. Is it easier to invert $A$ or $B$?
> > >
> > > > [!def] Schor Compliment of ($A$ in $D$)
> > > > Define the following compliments:
> > > >
> > > > $$
> > > > \begin{align}
> > > > S = B - RA^{-1}C \\
> > > > T = A - CB^{-1}R
> > > > \end{align}
> > > > $$
> > >
> > > **_We can consider two factorizations._**
> > >
> > > $$
> > > 	\begin{bmatrix}
> > > 		A & C \\
> > > 		R & B
> > > \end{bmatrix}^{-1} =
> > > 	\begin{cases}
> > > 		\begin{bmatrix}
> > > 			A^{-1}+A^{-1}CS^{-1}RA & -ACS^{-1} \\
> > > 			-S^{-1}RA^{-1}         & S^{-1}
> > > \end{bmatrix}           & \text{ When A is invertible} \\
> > >                                                               \\
> > > 		\begin{bmatrix}
> > > 			T^{-1}         & -T^{-1}CB^{-1}              \\
> > > 			-B^{-1}RT^{-1} & B^{-1}+B^{-1}RT^{-1}CB^{-1}
> > > \end{bmatrix} \quad & \text{When B is invertible}
> > > \end{cases}
> > > $$

# Rectangular Systems and Echelon Form

Recall: Number of Solutions

> [!case] In order: Square, Fat-Short, Tall-Skinny
> $$
> 	\begin{align}
> 		 \begin{bmatrix} x & x \\ x & x \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} x \\ x \end{bmatrix}
> 		\quad \quad \quad
> 		 \begin{bmatrix} x & x & x \end{bmatrix}      \begin{bmatrix} x \\ x \\ x \end{bmatrix}
> 		\quad \quad \quad
> 		 \begin{bmatrix} x \\ x \\ x \end{bmatrix}    \begin{bmatrix} x \end{bmatrix}
> 		\\
> 	\end{align}
> $$
> 1. Tall-Skinny
>     - No Solutions (Too many constraints)
>     - Can only find least-squared solutions "approximations"
> 
> 2. Square
>     - Singular solution
>         - Cholesky, LU Factor, Itterative Solvers, etc.
> 3. Fat-Short
>     - Infinitely Many Solutions
