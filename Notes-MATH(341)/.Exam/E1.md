## Example: Applying Gram-Schmitt

Apply Gram Scmitt to the columns of A

$$
	\begin{gathered}
		A =
		\begin{bmatrix}
			1 & -4 \\
			2 & 3  \\
			2 & 2
		\end{bmatrix} = [\vec{v_1}, \vec{v_2}] \\ \\
		\underbrace{\left\| v_1 \right\|}_{r_{1,1}} = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 3^2} = 3 \implies q_1 = \frac{1}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 3 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{v_1}{\left\| v_1 \right\|} \\
		q_2 = \frac{v_2 - \underbrace{(q_1^Tv_2)}_{r_{1,2}}q_1}{ \underbrace{\left\| v_2 - (q_1^Tv_2)q_1 \right\|}_{r_{2,2}} } \\ \\
		\tiny{\text{$v_2 - (q_1^Tv_2)q_1$ Descibes the components of $v_2$ that point along the same direction of $v_1$}} \\
		v_2 - (q_1^Tv_2)q_1 = \begin{bmatrix}
			-4 \\ 3 \\ 2
		\end{bmatrix} -
		\underbrace{\frac{1}{3}
			\left(
			\begin{bmatrix} 1 &  2 &  2 \end{bmatrix}
			\begin{bmatrix} -4 \\ 3 \\ 2 \end{bmatrix}
			\right)}_{r_{1,2}=2} \cdot
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\frac{1}{3} \\
			\frac{2}{3} \\
			\frac{3}{3}
		\end{bmatrix} =
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\frac{-14}{3} \\ \frac{5}{3} \\ \frac{2}{3}
		\end{bmatrix} \\ \\
		\implies Q =
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\frac{1}{3} & \frac{-14}{15} \\
			\frac{2}{3} & \frac{2}{3}    \\
			\frac{2}{5} & \frac{2}{15}
		\end{bmatrix} \quad
		R =
		\begin{bmatrix}
			3 & 2 \\ 0 &  5
		\end{bmatrix} \implies  A=QR \\\\
		\text{If we want to find full $QR$, we can take a random vector say } \begin{bmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} \text{ and use it as $v_3$}: \\
q_3 = \frac{v_3-(q_1^Tv_3)q_1-(q_2^Tv_3)q_2}{\left\| v_3-(q_1^Tv_3)q_1-(q_2^Tv_3)q_2 \right\|} \\ \\
QR =
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} q_1 &  q_2 &  q_3 \end{bmatrix}}_{Q}
\underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 3 &  2 \\ 0 &  5 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{R} = A
	\end{gathered}
$$
